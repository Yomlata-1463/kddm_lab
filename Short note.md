Short note

In "customers", "customer_id" is issued per order, while "customer_unique_id" identifies the real person and repeats across orders , so I counted customers with "customer_unique_id". "order_items" is the junction table: its composite key ("order_id", "order_item_id") lets one order hold many products and sellers, so it resolves the many-to-many relationships between orders and products and between orders and sellers. The data is stricter than my diagram for "customers" -> "orders" every "customer_id" has exactly one order, because a new "customer_id" is issued for each order, but I still drew "many" because a real customer ("customer_unique_id") can place several orders. The circle on "orders" -> "order_items" is justified. 775 orders have no items mostly unavailable and canceled so a parent can exist without children.
